# Blog plan: first 10 articles

## Why these ten and not the obvious ten

The obvious plan is ten condition explainers. "What is diabetes", "Symptoms of
thyroid", "Causes of back pain". Do not write those.

Two things killed that plan. Google's December 2025 core update cut health
publishers badly, with WebMD down roughly 43% from peak and Healthline close to
half, and those sites had flawless credentials behind every article. And clinical
queries now return an AI Overview 89% to 100% of the time, so even a page that
ranks often never gets the click.

Three kinds of query still pay:

| Query type | AI Overview rate | What it means for us |
|---|---|---|
| Cost | 80%+ | We get quoted constantly, and almost nobody in India writes these |
| Local | ~0% | No AI Overview at all, the click comes straight to us |
| Our own procedures | n/a | Nobody else can write it, so nobody else competes |

All ten below sit in one of those three. None of them competes with Healthline.

## Rules that apply to every article

**Open with the answer.** First paragraph gives the number, the timing or the
direct yes/no. No welcome, no mission statement. Google's rater guidelines treat
filler above the main content as a quality demotion in its own right, so this
helps twice.

**Headings are questions.** "What does cataract surgery cost in Chennai" beats
"Cost factors". Question headings are what answer engines lift.

**Numbers go in tables.** Prose numbers do not get extracted. Tables do.

**Every paragraph stands alone.** Repeat "AJSMC" and "Egmore, Chennai" naturally
through the piece. An AI engine quotes one paragraph, not the page.

**Byline with the TNMC number** on anything clinical. Cheapest credibility signal
we have, and our consultants already have the registration.

**Never write** cure, permanent relief, reversal, guaranteed, 100%, or any success
rate. The Drugs and Magic Remedies Act schedule covers 54 conditions including
diabetes, cataract, heart disease, blood pressure, kidney stones, hernia, piles and
infertility, which is most of what we treat. "Treatment for X is available" is
fine. "We cure X" is not.

**Add a visible published date and a separate last reviewed date.** Only bump the
reviewed date when the content genuinely changed.

treats as a violation, so this group is safe and overdue. All three should link to

## Group D: symptom guidance, doctor authored

This group is permitted specifically because IMC reg. 7.11 allows a physician to
write on "matters of public health, hygienic living" while it prohibits writing that
advertises the physician. Keep these educational. The moment one reads like a pitch
it stops being protected.

Each must carry the author's name and TNMC number, and each ends by saying when to
see a doctor rather than by selling a package.
