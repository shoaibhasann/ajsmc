# Blog plan: replacement

## What changed

The client has decided that no fees, prices, charges, rupee figures or price bands
appear anywhere on the website. That is final, and it is not negotiable at the
margins either — no "starting from", no bands, no comparative claims like "costs
less than an admission".

The old plan's Group A was three cost articles and a fees page they all linked
into. That group is deleted. The hub is deleted. One third of the founding thesis
table — the row that said cost queries are where a new domain wins — is gone with
it.

What that actually costs us is smaller than it looks, and the reason is
uncomfortable. Two of the three cost articles were never winnable. Page one for
"master health checkup chennai" is nine hospital-owned pages — Apollo ProHealth,
Kauvery, SRM Global, Sri Ramachandra, Mehta, Prashanth, Hande, Anderson — with
ten to twenty-five years of domain history and full test lists already published.
The stated gap, that listings show a price with no test list, does not exist. And
the claim that very few Chennai hospitals publish a sleep study price is simply
wrong: Bookmerilab, Srivari, Sleep Blizz, Oxygen Times, MyTracky and RespBuy all
run dedicated Chennai sleep-price pages, alongside Apollo, Kauvery and Fortis
Malar sleep centres. That article was lost to a research error months before the
client made any decision. Say that plainly rather than letting them carry it.

Honest number: the ban costs roughly 10-15% of realistic year-one organic
sessions. What it genuinely costs is conversion, not traffic. Price was the only
bottom-of-funnel content in the plan. Everything left is mid-funnel education that
converts through a phone call, which lengthens the path — call it a 25-35% hit to
conversion efficiency. Budget for that, and instrument the helpline so "call for a
quote" is measurable, or nobody will ever be able to prove the content worked.

One open question worth asking the client before anything else: does "no prices"
mean no rupee figures, or no commercial content at all? If it is the former, then
insurance and TPA content is available to us and it is a better replacement for
the cost wedge than anything else in this document. "Is cataract surgery covered
by insurance in Chennai", "cashless hospitals in Egmore", "which TPAs does this
hospital accept" are high-intent, bottom-funnel, near-zero AI Overview, and none
of them requires a number. Get a ruling on this.

## What wins instead

The number in the first paragraph is now a duration, an hour, a cycle day, a dose
interval or an age band. Same tactic, different unit.

Three things carry the plan:

| What | Why it holds without a price |
|---|---|
| Operational protocol | The fasting cut-off, the cycle day a hormone must be drawn on, the rule that a late vaccine dose resumes rather than restarts. Patients get these wrong constantly and no aggregator can invent them about our building. |
| Local | Local health queries return an AI Overview about 11% of the time against roughly 93% for condition queries. The click still lands on us. |
| Entity clarity | Branded mentions and entity clarity correlate with AI citation. Domain authority does not. Twenty-eight named consultants are twenty-eight entity nodes we already own. |

One warning that applies to all of it. A price is a quote. An operational number is
a written service commitment. "Reports by 4pm" and "discharge by 6pm" are promises
a hospital with no content team has no process to keep current, and a patient who
misses a train because a published cut-off was stale has a grievance a price never
creates. Publish intervals and sequences. Avoid hard clock times. Every page that
carries one gets a named owner and a review date.

### The fourth thing, and it may be the best of them: the free camps

AJSMC runs free medical camps and intends to promote them. Nothing else in this
document comes close to it, and it was missing from every earlier draft because
nobody had mentioned it.

Why it beats the cost wedge we lost. A camp is a real event with a date, a place
and a subject, which is the one content type where a new domain is not competing
against Healthline at all — there is no national page for "free diabetes screening
camp in Egmore on the 14th". It carries local intent, which barely triggers an AI
Overview. It is the only bottom-of-funnel content left to us: people who attend a
camp are already in the building. And it generates the thing the research said
actually correlates with being cited by answer engines — branded mentions off our
own domain, in local press, in resident association posts, in community groups —
which no amount of blogging produces.

It also solves the fees problem cleanly rather than working around it. The client's
reasoning for not publishing charges is competitive, not evasive: patients are told
everything before treatment starts and nothing is hidden once they are here. A camp
page states that position in the only form that needs no number — free means free,
and the page can say exactly what is included, who should come, what to bring and
what happens if something is found.

What to build, once, then repeat per camp:

- **A standing `/camps` page.** Upcoming camps at the top, past camps below with
  what was screened and how many attended. The archive is what makes the page rank
  between events instead of dying to a 404 each time.
- **A page per camp**, published at least two weeks ahead so it has time to index:
  date, timing, venue, which screenings, which consultant is attending, who it is
  for, what to bring, whether to fast, and how to register. Target queries look
  like `free health checkup camp chennai`, `free diabetes screening egmore`,
  `free eye camp chennai`.
- **A follow-up section on each camp page after it runs** — what was found in
  aggregate, with no identifying detail, and what the next step is for someone who
  was told to come back. That is first-party data nobody else has.

Two cautions. Do not publish attendee photographs without written consent, and
never with a name attached to a finding. And do not let a camp page drift into
advertising the hospital's results — reporting "we screened 300 people" is a fact;
"we cured" or "we detected more than anyone else" is the boasting that IMC reg.
6.1.1 prohibits and that took the testimonials off the homepage.

This should be scheduled ahead of most of Group B.

## Rules that apply to every article

**Open with the answer.** First paragraph gives the number, the timing or the
direct yes/no. No welcome, no mission statement. Filler above the main content is
a quality demotion in its own right.

**The number is never money.** Hours, days, visits, doses, millilitres, cycle
days, age bands. Anything with a rupee sign in it does not ship.

**Where cost is the honest next question, say so and stop.** "Call 044 2532 2021
and you will be quoted before you decide." The site already promises upfront
quoting in the FAQ. That is a process promise, not a figure, and it is compliant.

**Headings are questions.** "Which dengue test do I need on day 3 of fever" beats
"Test selection". Question headings are what answer engines lift.

**Numbers go in tables.** Prose numbers do not get extracted. Tables do.

**Every paragraph stands alone.** Repeat "AJSMC" and "Egmore, Chennai" naturally
through the piece. An AI engine quotes one paragraph, not the page.

**Byline with the registration number** on anything clinical. Eight of the authors
below do not currently have one recorded. That is the real publishing blocker.
Ms. Chynna Christina is a counselling psychologist — RCI, not TNMC, and she is
never written as "Dr".

**Never imply emergency capability.** No casualty unit, no trauma unit, no
cardiologist. Wherever a red flag is discussed, the 108 line goes in the body,
near the flag, not in a footer. And we do not compete to be *found* by an
emergency searcher — see the safety vetoes.

**Never write** cure, permanent relief, reversal, guaranteed, 100%, or any success
or survival rate. The Drugs and Magic Remedies Act schedule covers diabetes,
cataract, blood pressure, kidney stones, hernia, piles and infertility, which is
most of what we treat.

**Describe only what the building has.** If site.ts does not evidence the machine
or the consultant, the article does not describe the service. This is the rule the
previous draft broke most often.

**Visible published date and a separate last reviewed date.** Only bump reviewed
when the content genuinely changed. Add a named owner on any page carrying an
operational number.

## Dropped, and why

### The five safety vetoes

**1. Breast screening — dropped entirely from wave one. Do not re-angle it.**
Mammography is unverified, but the deeper problem is the byline. Publishing
"Breast Screening in Chennai" under a surgical oncologist advertises a breast
service consisting of two oncologists, no evidenced mammography, no radiotherapy,
no evidenced chemotherapy day unit and no tumour board. A woman with a suspicious
finding arrives expecting AJSMC to handle it. "Biopsy referred out" in paragraph
nine does not undo what the title and byline imply. **Topic 21 does not ship.**

**2. The entire day-care surgery cluster.** site.ts publishes General Surgery as a
specialty advertising hernia, appendicitis, gallstones, piles and lumps as
day-care procedures. There is no general surgeon on the roster — nineteen distinct
specialties appear in the doctor list and General Surgery is not one of them. The
old plan's article 6 is bylined "General Surgery consultant", a person who does
not exist. Five proposed day-care articles and that one all hold until either a
surgeon is missing from the data or the specialty comes off the site.

**3. "Chest discomfort" comes out of every heading, title and target query.** An
earlier draft had the routing article's first H2 read "which doctor do I see for
stomach pain, chest discomfort, or body pain", bylined by the consultant whose
role string reads "General Physician & Emergency Medicine", on a site with no
cardiologist and no casualty. The 108 line does not fix that — the problem is that
we would be competing to be *found* by that searcher. The route-out statement stays
in body copy where it does not attract the query, and the router is re-bylined.

**4. The generic heat stroke versus heat exhaustion explainer.** Topic 10 as
written routes a collapsing patient toward a hospital with no casualty unit.

service? Nothing in site.ts evidences either. B1, B2 and C7 change or die on the

**Resolve General Surgery.** It is published as a specialty offering hernia,
appendicitis, gallstones and piles as day-care, with zero surgeons on the roster.
Either a surgeon is missing from the data or the specialty should not be on the
site. Say which.

**Collect the seventeen missing registration numbers,** starting with Sarvath,
Pradeep Sathya, Naveen Kumar, Fahadh, Karthikeyan, Arun, Bharathi Sezhian, and

contains nineteen. Oncology, Radiology, Pulmonology, Neurology, Nephrology,

Surgery is published but unstaffed. The whole plan promises entity clarity as its
