# Medical fact-check report

**Verdict: NEEDS_REWORK** — neither draft may be published in its current form.

## Blocking issues

1. A1, HPV table — upper age bound is wrong or unverifiable. The published IAP-ACVIP 2025 abstract (Indian Pediatrics 2026;63:311-324) reads 'Immunocompetent girls aged 9 to 15 years may receive a single dose.' The draft prints '9 to under 15 years' for the single dose and then routes 'anyone starting at 15 years or older' to a three-dose schedule. Those two readings give a 15-year-old girl completely different treatment. Reconcile against the full-text table before publication — do not publish either version on the abstract alone.

2. A1, typhoid conjugate row — '6-9 months, single 0.5 mL intramuscular dose, no booster' is not supported by any source I could reach. ACVIP's own material states IAP recommends typhoid vaccine 'from 9 months onward', noting only that the conjugate vaccine *can* be given from 6 months. Printing 6-9 months as the scheduled age will send parents to ask for a dose earlier than IAP schedules it. Replace with the age the 2025 table actually prints (verify: likely 9-12 months) or delete the row.

3. A1, IPV reconciliation sentence — 'A child who completed the government fractional series does not need an extra IPV dose at 16-18 months, but does still need an intramuscular IPV booster at 4-6 years (IAP-ACVIP, Indian Pediatrics e-pub January 2024)'. I could not confirm IAP says this. The sentence deletes a scheduled booster dose on an unverifiable citation, which is a missed-dose risk in the one direction that cannot be undone. Quote the exact IAP sentence with page, or cut it and write 'ask the paediatrician to reconcile the two schedules dose by dose'.

4. A1, 'Which reactions need a doctor the same day?' table — listing 'Fever that is not settling: axillary 38 C (100.4 F) or above, or the child feels hot to touch' alongside anaphylaxis, seizure and hypotonic hyporesponsive episode is clinically wrong and self-contradicting. The same article states fever occurs in about 1 in 10 children and up to 1 in 2 after a DTP or Td booster, and is managed at home. As drafted the page tells roughly half of booster recipients to seek same-day review, and points them at a centre with no casualty unit. Replace the row with real red flags: infant under 3 months, temperature above 40 C, fever beginning more than 48 hours after the dose or lasting beyond 48-72 hours, or fever with drowsiness, poor feeding, breathing difficulty or a fit.

5. A1 — '[CONFIRM: post-vaccination observation practice and AEFI management arrangements at AJSMC]' cannot be left open at publication. This page invites parents to bring infants for immunisation while the article itself states that MoHFW requires immediate intramuscular adrenaline for anaphylaxis and that AJSMC has no casualty unit. Written confirmation that adrenaline and an AEFI kit are stocked, in date, and that staff are trained to use them is a precondition for the page existing, not a footnote to be resolved later.

6. A2 — no byline. The article carries no author line and no registration number anywhere at the top, and closes with a [CONFIRM] naming Dr D. Divya Dakshani, TNMC 130971, who is not the verified author supplied for this piece. Per the roster, A2 must be bylined Dr Ashutosh Kumar Singh, Consultant General Physician, TNMC 126451, with the paediatric paracetamol paragraph reviewed by Dr A. Muhammed Shadique, TNMC 111008. Do not put the unnamed-author version in front of a consultant for sign-off.

7. A2, capability overclaim in 'Which of these tests are run at the AJSMC laboratory in Egmore?' — 'the complete blood count with haematocrit and platelet count ... is done here and repeated here when serial monitoring is needed' offers serial monitoring of a febrile dengue patient at an outpatient centre with no admission capability, no casualty and no evidenced 24-hour laboratory. The article's own text says the critical phase begins after day 3-4 and needs a hospital. Add the boundary in the same paragraph: serial counts only for a patient well enough for outpatient care, and anyone with a warning sign, or a rising haematocrit with a falling platelet count, goes to a hospital that can admit.

8. A1, invented operational detail presented as fact — three instances that the brief does not evidence: 'The paediatric consultation number is 044 2532 2021' (the brief gives one general hospital number, not a paediatric line); 'This is the single most common worry parents bring to the paediatric clinic at AJSMC in Egmore, Chennai' (an unverifiable claim about AJSMC's own caseload); and 'It is the schedule Dr. Shadique works from at AJSMC in Egmore, Chennai' (his personal practice is not evidenced). Convert each to a [CONFIRM] or to neutral wording.

## Corrections

1. A1, four places: '9-11 months' -> '9-12 months'. The National Immunization Schedule gives MR-1, the PCV booster, fIPV-3 and JE-1 at 9 completed months to 12 months. Affects the 9-month table row, the 'Injectable polio' row ('6 weeks, 14 weeks and 9-11 months'), the 15-month row ('given at 9-11 months in the programme') and the MR row of the free/paid table.

2. A1, three places: '16-23 months' -> '16-24 months'. DPT booster-1, MR-2, the OPV booster and JE-2 are all scheduled at 16-24 months in the National Immunization Schedule.

3. A1, Vitamin A row: 'doses 2 to 9 from 16-23 months with MR-2 and then six-monthly to 5 years' -> 'dose 2 at 16-18 months, then one dose every six months to the age of 5'. The NIS prints 16-18 months for dose 2, not 16-24.

4. A1, second table intro: delete 'because parents in Chennai are frequently given an optional vaccine privately when the same protection was available free that week, or told a vaccine is free when it is not.' It is a comparative cost framing and it imputes misconduct to other providers (IMC reg. 7.11 exposure). Replace with: 'The government programme and the IAP-ACVIP chart do not line up dose for dose, so it is worth knowing which of the doses due at a given age the programme provides.'

5. A1, HPV free/paid row: 'Free for girls aged 14 only' is too fixed. The 28 February 2026 national launch also covered girls turning 15 within 90 days of launch, ran as a 90-day intensive drive delivered largely through schools with U-WIN registration, and then transitions to an annual programme. Reword to 'girls aged 14, with the launch cohort also covering girls turning 15 within 90 days of 28 February 2026' and keep the existing [CONFIRM] against TN DPH for state delivery.

6. A1: the article prints two fever thresholds - 'Fever above 38.5 C on the day' (postpone the dose) and '38 C (100.4 F) or above' (be seen). Label each with its context in the sentence itself ('on the day of the appointment, to decide whether to give the dose' vs 'after the dose'), or a parent reads them as competing numbers.

7. A1, egg allergy row: 'Only yellow fever vaccine is contraindicated' reads as a blanket green light. Add 'a history of anaphylaxis to any vaccine component must still be told to the doctor before the dose.'

8. A1, closing: 'Reviewed by Dr. A. Muhammed Shadique' names the same person as the author. Add a second, independent reviewer - Dr Ashutosh Kumar Singh, Consultant General Physician, TNMC 126451.

9. A2: reconcile the chikungunya IgM window. The staging table quotes NCDC+ICMR (Nov 2025) putting chikungunya IgM serology at 'more than 5 days', while the test-window table quotes NCVBDC 2023 at 'after day 7 / not useful in first 7 days'. Both quotes are accurate - I verified the NCVBDC wording verbatim ('Not useful in first 7 days of illness'; 'RT PCR between days D0 and D7 and serology after D7'). Add one sentence saying the two national documents differ and that a negative IgM before day 7 does not exclude chikungunya.

10. A2: reconcile the dengue rash timing. The differential table says 'appears after day 3-4' (verified: NCVBDC Dengue Guidelines 2023, febrile phase - 'usually appears after the 3rd to 4th day of fever') while the chikungunya comparison says dengue rash appears 'between days 5-7' (verified: NCVBDC Chikungunya 2023, Table 3). Print one figure and footnote the other, rather than both without comment.

11. A2, platelet paragraph: 'there is no role for preventive platelet transfusion' overstates the source. NCVBDC 2023 says platelet transfusion is not recommended for thrombocytopenia alone and that there is no prophylactic transfusion, but it also allows prophylaxis to 'be considered' below 10,000/cumm without bleeding, and in adults with hypertension and platelets under 10,000. Reword to 'not recommended for a low platelet count alone - the narrow exceptions are decided in hospital.'

12. A2, papaya sentence: 'they are unproven, not simply harmless' asserts harm with no source. I confirmed papaya/Carica appears nowhere in the NCVBDC 2023 guideline, so state exactly that: 'no national guideline recommends them, and they are not a substitute for being reviewed.'

13. A2, paracetamol bullet: add 'this is the dengue-specific instruction in NCVBDC 2023' to the sentence. The A1 page quotes MoHFW at 10-15 mg/kg every 4-6 hours, maximum four doses in 24 hours; A2 quotes NCVBDC at 10 mg/kg at intervals of not less than six hours, maximum 4 g/day in adults. I verified the A2 wording verbatim against the guideline (including 'body temperature should be kept below 100 F'), so both are correct in context - but a reader on both pages sees two regimens.

14. A2, epidemiology table: the 2023 Tamil Nadu dengue row is footnoted 'provisional till 28 Feb 2026'. That caveat belongs only on the 2026 row.

15. A2: reviewer should tick the leptospirosis test-window rows (PCR, blood culture within 10 days, IgM from late in week 1, MAT paired sera, urine culture day 10-30) and the mortality claim ('over 90% of deaths from pulmonary bleeding and kidney failure') line by line against the NCDC 2015 PDF - I could not retrieve the text to verify them. The same applies to the Cureus 2026 Widal figures, the Lancet Regional Health typhoid modelling, and the TN DPH IDSP-IHIP leptospirosis percentages.

16. Verified and needing no change, so the consultant does not re-litigate them: NS1 day 1-5 and IgM after day 5, and the instruction to record day of onset on the request form (NCVBDC 2023, section 6.2); RDTs not recommended by the programme; CBC with haematocrit and platelet count in all patients with fever more than 3 days and all patients with warning signs; critical phase in 5-10% beginning after the 3rd or 4th day; the home-care paracetamol and 100 F wording; the dengue warning-sign list; the 13 Tamil Nadu JE-endemic districts with Chennai excluded; the third fractional IPV dose at 9 months (introduced January 2022); the trivalent influenza switch, nirsevimab restriction to high-risk infants, and the five-dose day 0/3/7/14/28 rabies PEP schedule in IAP-ACVIP 2025.
