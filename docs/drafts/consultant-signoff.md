### 1.1 TYPHOID CONJUGATE VACCINE AGE — does IAP-ACVIP 2025 schedule TCV at 6-9 months as a single 0.5 mL IM dose with no booster, or from 9 months onward? Is a booster recommended?

**Finding (CONFIRMED):** The draft is CORRECT and the fact-checker was wrong. I retrieved the full text of IAP-ACVIP 2025 (Indian Pediatrics 2026;63:311-324). Table 2 (IAP-ACVIP Immunization Timetable 2025: Vaccines for Routine Use, p.313) prints the row verbatim as: age '6-9 months' | vaccine 'Typhoid Conjugate Vaccine' | comment 'There is no recommendation for a booster dose'. The narrative section 'Typhoid Conjugate Vaccine (TCV) — Single dose of TCV' (p.314) states verbatim: 'The IAP-ACVIP had recommended single dose of TCV vaccine, 0.5 mL, by intramuscular (IM) route, from 6 months onward, in its earlier guidelines' and the boxed recommendation reads 'The IAP-ACVIP recommends continuation of single dose (0.5 mL, IM) of TCV vaccine from 6 months of age.' The stated rationale is 7-year follow-up data in the 6-23 month cohort showing non-significant title differences between boosted and non-boosted groups (Vadrevu et al, Vaccine 2021;39:6682-90), plus heterogeneity of enteric fever incidence in India and no robust evidence of waning immunity after a single dose. The checker's 'from 9 months onward' figure comes from ACVIP's older parent-facing web column (acvip.org), not from the 2025 schedule, and is superseded. So: age 6-9 months correct, single dose correct, 0.5 mL IM correct, no booster correct. Separate caveat, outside this question: the draft's adjacent free/paid cell ('TCV is not yet part of the national routine schedule') could not be confirmed against a primary MoHFW source — NTAGI recommended TCV for UIP introduction in 2022 and Gavi funding references introducing TCV into the routine schedule, but I found no MoHFW/PIB notification of a completed national rollout. That cell should be re-checked separately before publication.

**Proposed wording:** Typhoid conjugate vaccine — a single 0.5 mL intramuscular dose from 6 months of age, scheduled at 6-9 months. No booster dose is recommended (IAP-ACVIP 2025, Indian Pediatrics 2026;63:311-324, Table 2 and the TCV recommendation).

**Decision:**  ☐ accept as written   ☐ amend (write below)   ☐ cut the claim


### 1.2 HPV SINGLE-DOSE UPPER AGE BOUND — is it '9 to under 15 years' or '9 to 15 years', what exactly happens at 15 and above, and what is the free-vaccine position now that the 90-day national drive has ended?

**Finding (CONFIRMED):** The draft is CORRECT and the abstract is the loose text, not the other way round. IAP-ACVIP 2025 (Indian Pediatrics 2026;63:311-324) prints the bound as 'up to 15' in every place where the schedule is actually specified. Table 2 (p.313) row: '9-up to 15 years | HPV | Immunocompetent girls—single dose 9-up to 15 years of age (boys): Two-dose schedule (0.5 mL at 0 and 6 months)'. The next row: '15-18 years | HPV | 15-26 years of age (females and males) and immunocompromised: 3-dose (0.5 mL at 0, 2, and 6 months) schedule'. Table 1 (Changes in the IAP-ACVIP Immunization Guidelines 2025, p.312): 'IAP-ACVIP recommends that immunocompetent girls of 9 up to 15 years can be given a single dose of HPV vaccine for sustained protection' and 'For children more than 15 years, and immunocompromised children of any age, three doses are to be given'. Fig. 1 footnote (j), p.315: 'From 15th year onward and in immunocompromised subjects at all ages, HPV vaccines are recommended as a 3-dose schedule, 0-2-6 months.' Fig. 1's own age column header is '9-<15Y'. The IAP-ACVIP Recommendations box (p.318) repeats '9-up to 15 years can be given a single IM dose'. The published abstract's 'aged 9 to 15 years' is shorthand and is contradicted by four separate specifications in the same paper; do not follow it. So a girl who has turned 15 gets the 3-dose 0-2-6 schedule, not a single dose — the draft's routing is right. On the free-vaccine position: PIB backgrounder of 28 February 2026 states the free programme covers girls aged 14, that 'Girls who turn 15 within 90 days of the launch will also be eligible under the intensive three-month campaign', that the excluded group includes 'Girls outside the 14-year target age group', and that 'The 90-day vaccination drive will run daily to ensure maximum coverage. Thereafter, the vaccine will continue to be available on routine immunisation days.' The MoHFW Rajya Sabha reply of 17 March 2026 (PIB release 2241079) confirms: 'The single-dose Gardasil-4 vaccine is administered free of cost at government health facilities... After the campaign duration of three-months, the vaccine will continue to be available on routine immunization days.' The 90-day window closed at the end of May 2026, so as of now the turning-15 extension has lapsed and free entitlement is girls aged 14 only, on routine immunisation days at government facilities.

**Proposed wording:** IAP-ACVIP 2025 gives immunocompetent girls a single dose of HPV vaccine from 9 years up to 15 years of age; boys in the same 9 to under 15 band get two doses at 0 and 6 months with at least a 5-month gap. From the 15th year onward, and for immunocompromised children at any age, the vaccine is given as three doses at 0, 2 and 6 months (IAP-ACVIP 2025, Indian Pediatrics 2026;63:311-324, Table 2 and Fig. 1 footnote j). Free of cost, the national programme covers girls aged 14 with a single dose of the quadrivalent vaccine at government health facilities; the launch drive from 28 February 2026 also covered girls turning 15 within 90 days of launch, but that 90-day campaign window has closed, and the vaccine now continues on routine immunisation days for the 14-year age group (MoHFW reply in Rajya Sabha, PIB, 17 March 2026).

**Decision:**  ☐ accept as written   ☐ amend (write below)   ☐ cut the claim


### 1.3 IPV RECONCILIATION — does a child who completed fractional IPV under the national programme need no extra IPV at 16-18 months but still need an IM IPV booster at 4-6 years?

**Finding (CONFIRMED):** CONFIRMED verbatim, and the citation should be upgraded from the e-pub to the paginated version. The source is IAP-ACVIP 2023, Indian Pediatrics 2024;61:113-125 (issue of 15 February 2024), section '3. Injectable Polio Vaccine — Guidance on Changeover from NIP to IAP Schedule', page 118. The boxed 'IAP-ACVIP Recommendations for IPV' on p.118 reads verbatim: 'For infants who have received the 3-dose fIPV as per the NIP, an additional dose of IM-IPV is not necessary at 16-18 mo.' and 'A booster of IM-IPV is recommended at 4-6 y. The rationale for a booster at 4-6 y has been published earlier.' Box I on p.114 states it more plainly: 'A child, who has received 3 doses of fIPV at 6w-14w-9 mo, does not need an additional dose of IM-IPV. However, the child should receive a 2nd booster of IM-IPV at 4-6 y.' The stated basis is that there is no substantial difference in seroconversion between 2 and 3 doses of intradermal fIPV and 2 and 3 doses of full-dose IM-IPV, so an additional IM-IPV dose would be of marginal benefit only. One load-bearing condition the draft must carry: this applies only to a child who received all three fIPV doses of the NIP series (6 weeks, 14 weeks and 9 months). For a child with only two fIPV doses the earlier guidance stands — an additional full-dose IM-IPV at least 8 weeks after the last fIPV dose. IAP-ACVIP 2025 does not revisit this changeover guidance, and its own Table 2 still schedules IPV-B1 at 16-18 months and IPV-B2 at 4-6 years for the full IAP schedule, so the 2023 guidance is current.

**Proposed wording:** The IAP schedule uses full-dose intramuscular IPV at 6, 10 and 14 weeks. A child who completed all three fractional IPV doses of the government series does not need an additional intramuscular IPV dose at 16-18 months, but does still need an intramuscular IPV booster at 4-6 years (IAP-ACVIP 2023, Indian Pediatrics 2024;61:113-125, page 118). A child who received only two fractional doses is not covered by that reconciliation and should have the remaining doses worked out with the paediatrician.

**Decision:**  ☐ accept as written   ☐ amend (write below)   ☐ cut the claim

### Editorial note worth knowing

WHO replaced its dengue guidance on 4 July 2025 (*Guidelines for clinical management of arboviral
diseases: dengue, chikungunya, Zika and yellow fever*). Anything still citing 'WHO 2009' as the
live position is out of date. Both articles cite WHO 2025 as current and use WHO 2009 only for
pathophysiology it uniquely describes.
